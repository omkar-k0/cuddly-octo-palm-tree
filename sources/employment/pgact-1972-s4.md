Gratuity is payable to an employee when their employment ends after they
have rendered continuous service of not less than five years, whether the
employment ends on superannuation, retirement, resignation, or death or
disablement due to accident or disease. The five-year condition does not
apply where employment ends because of death or disablement.

For every completed year of service, or part of a year in excess of six
months, the employer pays gratuity at the rate of fifteen days' wages based
on the rate of wages last drawn. The amount is subject to a ceiling notified
by the Central Government.

Gratuity may be wholly or partly forfeited if the employee's services were
terminated for an act, wilful omission or negligence causing damage or loss
to the employer's property (to the extent of the loss), or wholly forfeited
for riotous or disorderly conduct, violence, or an offence involving moral
turpitude committed in the course of employment.
